# Operator Feature Ideas

## Recommendation: Contract Preparation

The best next addition is a single decisive preparation choice before each contract proceeds. The prototype already has discovery, acceptance, a complication, and a resolution; preparation is the missing part of the intended loop.

Each authored contract should offer a small set of contract-specific approaches:

- **Go clean** — spend Credits on permits, documentation, or a safer route; reduce Heat exposure or preserve clean-resolution options.
- **Call in leverage** — use or create a contact favor; unlock a relationship-specific resolution.
- **Go cheap or fast** — preserve Credits and time, but remove the safest response when trouble occurs.

The later complication should reflect the committed preparation. This makes planning meaningful without adding an inventory, statistics, crew, or generic event-engine system.

Example:

> **Data Retrieval** — choose a clean access token for 400 CR, request Mara's routed vendor ID, or go in without cover. When the audit starts early, the available responses depend on that preparation choice.

## Follow-up Ideas

### 1. Make Heat active pressure

Heat should alter a small number of authored contract options rather than only record risky choices:

- Low Heat enables quiet or legitimate solutions.
- High Heat removes clean options but may unlock desperate or routed-contact solutions.
- Comms and the ticker acknowledge surveillance, audit attention, or exposure.

Keep this authored and local. Do not add a wanted-level simulation yet.

### 2. Expand favors into a small relationship economy

Generalize the existing Mara favor concept without creating a faction matrix:

- A contact can owe the player one favor.
- The player can owe a contact one favor.
- A favor unlocks or changes one specific authored contract choice or outcome.

This makes contacts mechanically meaningful while retaining the terminal-RPG focus.

### 3. Add contract aftermath

After resolving a job, present a concise follow-up message or event:

- A client recommends the player.
- An audit flag becomes a later complication.
- A clinic worker makes a personal request.
- A burned contact temporarily stops offering clean work.

This adds narrative consequence and makes the existing seven-contract portfolio feel larger before adding more contracts.

### 4. Add authored Lower Vesper districts

Add two or three local venues—not a map system—such as docks, transit exchange, clinic quarter, and harbor ward. Each can vary the backdrop, ticker language, contact context, and contract tone. This gives the world distinct places before off-world travel.

### 5. Add an operator dossier

Create a concise profile built from actions rather than starting stats:

- Known for clean work, aggressive work, or community work.
- Current contact standing and debts.
- Current Heat.
- Key completed operations.

This gives the player a readable record of the identity they have developed.

## Deliberately Deferred

Do not add these before the contract loop has more depth:

- Combat
- Generic inventory or equipment
- Crew recruitment and stats
- Market or trading system
- Procedural contracts
- Full faction reputation matrix
- Travel map and ships

The focused next step is **preparation plus consequential aftermath**. It deepens the existing player-facing loop while reusing the current contracts, Credits, Heat, contacts, favors, messages, and terminal UI.
